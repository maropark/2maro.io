Most "how scroll animation works" explainers are static articles with GIFs. KINESIS is the opposite — the explanation *is* the artifact. As you scroll, a fixed telemetry HUD names the technique you're currently experiencing — the glide, the type, the reveal, the turn, the contrast, the depth — alongside its mechanical spec and live readouts. You read what's happening, why it matters, and feel it happen, all at once.

The closing chapter is the thesis: the architecture is neutral, and the *register* is what you compose — the same six dials build a disorienting "maze" or a calm "breath." It's the generalised, teachable form of the architecture behind my Megumi redesign (itself lifted from a teardown of the Somni Restaurant site). Aurora Light aesthetic; plain HTML/CSS/JS plus a single vendored dependency (Lenis), no build step, and a first-class reduced-motion path that keeps naming every technique with the movement switched off.
